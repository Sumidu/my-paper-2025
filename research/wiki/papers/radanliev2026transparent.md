# Radanliev P. (2026)

The growing autonomy of agentic AI systems has intensified the need for robust frameworks of safety, accountability, and transparency. Unlike conventional AI models that execute pre-trained functions in a static manner, agentic AI systems exhibit goal-directed behaviour, long-term planning, and self-adaptive decision-making, thereby introducing new risks such as reward hacking, coordination drift, and opaque reasoning pathways. This chapter provides a critical examination of how the transition to agentic architectures, encompassing multi-agent systems, recursive reasoning loops, and evolving policy layers, demands safety mechanisms that go beyond traditional algorithmic audits. A taxonomy of agentic risks is presented alongside a conceptual framework rooted in transparency-by-design principles. The discussion explores the importance of traceable decision provenance, enhanced interpretability, and runtime behavioural observability within agentic environments. Drawing on examples of large language model agents, reinforcement learning-based policies, and distributed orchestration systems, the chapter outlines how transparency mechanisms can serve both preventative and forensic functions. Technical and regulatory instruments such as AI Bills of Materials (AIBOM), protocol-level logging, and agentic reasoning audit trails are examined as enablers of explainability and harm mitigation. The chapter concludes with a set of recommendations for embedding transparency and safety into the architectural fabric of agentic AI, highlighting directions for future research in AI alignment, adversarial testing, and accountability infrastructure. © 2026 selection and editorial matter, Tereza Raquel Merlo and Jay Liebowitz; individual chapters, the contributors.
